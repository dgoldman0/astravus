# Book I audio direction

The preview uses an original instrumental score and synthesized environmental sound. Every note, arrangement, and effect is generated by `scripts/make_audio.py`; there are no downloaded recordings, commercial samples, sampled voices, borrowed melodies, or Suno tracks. NumPy, SciPy, and SoundFile are build dependencies, not runtime dependencies. Music and ambience ship as Ogg Vorbis; five short effects remain PCM WAV. Ren'Py plays these files directly.

The original **First Light** motif remains recognizable: C–D–E–G–C–A–G–C. Its new arrangement adds answering phrases, overlapping harmony, a low register, and a small stereo room. The score shares this musical vocabulary across the book, but each cue changes its density, register, rhythm, and harmonic emphasis. This is intended to give scenes continuity without putting the same unchanging loop underneath every emotion.

## Cue sheet

| File in `game/audio/` | Length | Dramatic use and arrangement |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| `first_light.ogg` | 64 s | Welcome, First Breath, and the opening act of remembering. Gentle plucked motif, slowly changing bowed harmonies, and room for the prose. |
| `home_theme.ogg` | 66 s | The family home, meals, and ordinary affection. Lower felt-key melody, soft broken chords, and modest answering variations. |
| `discovery_theme.ogg` | 49 s | Workshop discoveries, friends' projects, and collaborative play. More movement in the plucked figures, with a lighter sustained layer. |
| `wonder_theme.ogg` | 77 s | Sage's story and the Tree of Echoes. Spacious suspended harmony and occasional quiet flute answers; no sudden orchestral reveal. |
| `festival_theme.ogg` | 46 s | Festival of Lights. A livelier plucked arrangement, answering flute, and restrained wooden pulse. The pulse supplies movement without turning the gathering into a march. |
| `rain_refuge.ogg` | 70 s | The shelter of the treehouse and quieter conversations. Sparse felt-key phrases with soft minor-coloured harmonies; rain remains a separate environmental layer. |
| `grief_theme.ogg` | 80 s | The aftermath of Joren's death. Long gaps in the melody, low soft support, and no swell or emphatic cadence. Let the news arrive in silence before using it. |
| `remembrance_theme.ogg` | 71 s | Shared remembering and the closing movement of Book I. Returns to First Light's contour with changed harmony and a quieter register. Affection and loss coexist; the arrangement avoids a victory finish. |

All music cues loop. The lengths above are rounded; the renderer places eight two-bar phrases on each cue's tempo grid. Phrases change across the loop rather than repeating one bar for a minute. Notes and reverb tails wrap into the start of the file. The loop therefore has no baked-in fade to silence; Ren'Py should fade music at entrances, exits, and scene changes.

| File | Length | Use |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| `garden_air.ogg` | 32 s loop | Filtered canopy air and small water sounds. No recognizable Earth bird calls. |
| `room_air.ogg` | 32 s loop | Very quiet interior air and the home's wall fountain. No mechanical ship hum. |
| `workshop_air.ogg` | 32 s loop | Quiet room sound with occasional small workbench taps. No heavy machinery. |
| `plaza_air.ogg` | 32 s loop | Soft cloth and movement texture for gatherings. It contains no speech or simulated spoken language. |
| `rain.ogg` | 32 s loop | A broad exterior rain layer with individually varied droplets. Start with the rain background and stop when the weather/setting changes. |
| `wood.wav` | 0.65 s | A small wooden contact, such as a ladder or treehouse movement. Avoid playing it on every click. |
| `tree_creak.wav` | 3.4 s | A low wooden flex for the Tree of Echoes. Not a voice, monster cue, or danger warning. |
| `water_splash.wav` | 2.2 s | A shallow splash for the pond incident. No implication of a deep-water drowning. |
| `flute_first.wav` | 8 s | Hesitant C–D–E and a returning C: separated breaths, less stable pitch, and an uncertain start. |
| `flute_practice.wav` | 12 s | A connected C–D–E–G–C–A–G–C phrase, showing musical progress through audible phrasing rather than narration alone. |

The flute sounds are story events. Fade out the background music before them; let each phrase continue across text where possible, then return to the score. Avoid a mandatory long wait that punishes a quick reader. Sound settings still govern these cues.

Music, ambience, and effects stay on their existing Ren'Py channels so the reader can adjust them. At the current default music volume of 0.32, the environmental layers are deliberately quiet, with rain more present than room tone. Keep silence available, especially at the death notification and the first stunned responses. A change of scene does not always need a change of music; change it when the emotional or physical setting warrants it.

## Synthesis and reproducibility

Run from `visual-novel/` with NumPy and SciPy available. Install the encoder in the ignored project cache (no global environment change and no FFmpeg dependency):

```sh
python3 -m pip install --target .cache/audio-tools soundfile==0.13.1
```

Then render and check:

```sh
python3 scripts/make_audio.py
python3 scripts/audio_check.py
```

To rebuild a selected asset without changing the others:

```sh
python3 scripts/make_audio.py first_light flute_first
```

Each asset has an independent fixed random seed. The thirteen long WAV masters are 24 kHz, 16-bit stereo and live only in ignored `.cache/audio-masters/`; the original draft First Light is preserved there as `first_light-draft-one.wav` for comparison. Runtime music and ambience use Ogg Vorbis at the same sample rate. The complete runtime set contains eight music cues, five ambient loops, and five short WAV effects, occupying **10.98 MiB**, about **83% smaller** than the 64.92 MiB PCM set. Track selected runtime audio and its source generator; keep masters, encoder dependencies, and measurements out of Git.

The validated encoder is SoundFile **0.13.1**, its bundled libsndfile **1.2.2**, and the Vorbis vendor string `Xiph.Org libVorbis I 20200704 (Reducing Environment)`. The renderer specifies OGG/VORBIS with [`compression_level=0.15`](https://python-soundfile.readthedocs.io/en/0.13.1/#controlling-bitrate-mode-and-compression-level), corresponding to quality 0.85 on libsndfile's 0–1 quality scale. The encoder chooses a variable bitrate. No resampling or loudness normalization happens during delivery encoding.

Ogg normally contains a random stream serial. The renderer replaces only that serial and the corresponding page CRCs with deterministic values following [Xiph's framing specification](https://xiph.org/ogg/doc/framing.html); compressed audio packets are unchanged. Re-encoding First Light from its cached master produces an identical runtime hash with the validated encoder. Encoder/library changes can still alter output and require revalidation. To re-encode cached masters without resynthesis, run `python3 scripts/make_audio.py --encode-existing`.

Plucked sounds use damped sinusoidal modes, slightly inharmonic upper partials, frequency-dependent decay, and low-level pick noise. Felt-key sounds use softly attacked paired strings with small detuning. Sustained layers add restrained vibrato and harmonics. Flute phrases combine harmonic tone, shaped breath noise, pitch settling, and phrasing. Stereo position uses constant-power panning, with a quiet generated reflection field rather than a sampled impulse response. Periodic filtering and wrapped events preserve loop continuity. The final conversion adds triangular dither and leaves ample headroom.

The approach to individual resonant modes is informed by Julius O. Smith's [Physical Audio Signal Processing: Modal Expansion](https://dsprelated.com/freebooks/pasp/Modal_Expansion.html). Filter implementation uses SciPy's documented [second-order-section filtering](https://docs.scipy.org/doc/scipy/reference/generated/scipy.signal.sosfilt.html). The arrangements and simplified instrument models here are original code; these references do not imply acoustically exact instrument reconstruction.

## Validation and limits

`audio_check.py` decodes the actual runtime Ogg/WAV files and validates all eighteen expected assets, codec/PCM format, nonzero content, DC offset, decoded clipping, four-times-oversampled peak estimates, stereo channel differences, music duration, and loop boundary steps relative to normal signal changes. When cached masters are present, it also verifies exact frame counts and measures level change and reconstruction error against those masters. It also reports SHA-256 hashes, half-second RMS variation, stereo correlation, spectral centroid, and energy by frequency band. Its JSON report is written to `test-results/audio-report.json`.

The current render passes all eighteen asset checks. Decoded music measures approximately −27.5 to −22.8 dBFS RMS, with sample peaks no higher than −7.95 dBFS. Quiet interior ambience is approximately −43 dBFS RMS; rain is approximately −34 dBFS RMS. Every decoded loop's boundary step is below its ordinary 99th-percentile sample step, and all files have negligible DC offset. Decoded frame counts match all thirteen masters exactly; average level changes are at most 0.134 dB. Music signal-to-reconstruction-error ratios are approximately 33–37 dB. Lossy coding changes individual noise samples in ambience more substantially; numerical reconstruction error is not itself an audibility measure. These measurements describe headroom and continuity; RMS is not a perceptual loudness rating.

This environment provides numerical audio analysis, not an audition that can be represented as human listening. The score has not been subjectively certified by hearing it here. In-game listening on speakers and headphones remains necessary to assess timbre, repetition, scene balance, and the flute's perceived expressiveness. The instruments and environments are deliberately stylized synthesis, not recorded performances or lifelike crowds. Their technical checks alone are not grounds to call the soundtrack final production audio.
