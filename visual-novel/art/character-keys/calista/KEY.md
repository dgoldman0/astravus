# Calista: childhood visual key

[Early key](early-key.png) · [Later key](later-key.png)

These sheets extend the selected Book I sprites into body turnarounds, facial angles and a small expression range. Both sheets depict childhood. The later sheet belongs after the manuscript's “As we grew older” transition, before Book II's late teens; it is not an adult design. Exact ages and physical heights are not newly assigned.

## Authority and identity

[Book I](../../../../revision/latest.md), especially the opening appearance description and the transition into projects, supplies stage and narrative context. [Calista's biography](../../../../wiki/bios/Calista.md) supplies the light olive complexion, nose/cheek freckles, chestnut hair and deep blue eyes. The selected [young](../../../game/images/characters/book-one/calista-young.png) and [older](../../../game/images/characters/book-one/calista-older.png) sprites anchor the actual face, hair arrangement and clothes. The [mourning sprite](../../../game/images/characters/book-one/calista-mourning.png) anchors restrained sadness.

Keep her softly rounded early face recognizable as it becomes modestly longer in later childhood. Preserve the freckles, small projecting nose, chin and blue irises when rotating her head. The existing expressive eye size is a style anchor; further enlargement into doll eyes is not part of this key. Her chestnut waves/curls and gathered crown distinguish her from Cassia's umber complexion, green eyes and longer flowing hair. Do not add her undated temple scar to these stages by assumption.

## Body, clothes and staging

Early play clothes remain a cream blouse, brown-olive overalls, dark bow and brown boots, with the selected sketchbook shown separately. Later exploration clothes remain the cream blouse, open olive pocket vest, slate trousers with brown knee patches and brown boots. These are production wardrobe choices, not manuscript specifications. The other selected home, festival, painting and mourning outfits remain valid activity variants; this sheet does not replace them with play clothes.

Use the game's existing relative standing heights: early Cali 675, Cassia 680 and Joren 710 stage pixels; later Cali 750, Cassia 755 and Joren 790. These are framing values, not centimetres. The individual sheets fill their own pages and must not be compared as a common physical scale. Later limbs are modestly longer and cheeks less round, while shoulders, hands and overall construction remain those of a child. A taller copy of the early body is insufficient.

## Expression and light

Early studies cover attentive curiosity, amusement, concentration and mild worry. Later studies add frustration and quiet sadness. Preserve her eye aperture and face structure when the brows and mouth move. Mourning remains closed-mouth and subdued; the amused face is not a default for loss scenes. Painting props belong only in painting staging, and a lantern only in the portion of the festival where she holds it.

The sheets use softly warm, diffuse studio light with readable skin midtones. Blue irises remain blue in warmer environments without becoming luminous; olive skin must not shift to Cassia's deeper umber or Joren's ruddy fairness. Match scene light across the whole face rather than recoloring an eye to overpower shadow.
