# Barkley: familiar visual key

[Visual key](key.png)

The sheet shows the selected family golden retriever in sitting front, standing side and rear views, three facial angles and coat/paw details. This is his existing Book I design, not a puppy or a newly aged variant.

## Sources and anatomy

[Book I](../../../../revision/latest.md) identifies Barkley as the golden retriever who shares the children's home and adventures. [Familiar lore](../../../../wiki/worldbuilding/Familiars.md) establishes golden fur, playfulness, loyalty and a wagging tail. The selected [sprite](../../../game/images/familiars/barkley.png) supplies his honey-gold feathered coat, wavy chest fur, floppy ears, brown eyes, broad retriever muzzle and full tail.

Preserve that exact retriever identity, four ordinary dog limbs and one naturally attached feathery tail. Keep the chest, loin and hock construction consistent across standing and sitting poses. Do not introduce white patches, a red coat, poodle curls, puppy proportions, collars or decorative equipment. Fine coat texture and a softly open relaxed mouth may vary with pose; the dog's identity may not.

## Scale, mood and light

Barkley is the largest of the three household familiars: rat smaller than cat smaller than dog. The sheet's scale reminder establishes only this ordering. He can settle his head against a child naturally, but the key does not assign centimetres, weight or an exact relationship to standing sprite heights.

Maintain alertness, affection and readiness to move through natural head/ear/body posture. An open mouth is a relaxed canine expression, not a human grin. Quieter scenes can use the selected closed-mouth attentive face. Do not let his usual playfulness force a smile into fearful or grieving staging.

Diffuse neutral light with softly warm highlights should preserve golden local color and clear fur structure. Avoid burning the highlights to white or making the coat uniformly orange. The original green background is technical chroma backing and must never become green light on his fur.

## Reference use

Use the standing and rear views to continue the golden coat and coherent single-tail anatomy. They introduce no new coat marks or later bond events.
