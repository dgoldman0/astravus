## Shoreline and section

The pond belongs within the paved garden. Its edge consists of broad, irregular stones whose upper surfaces continue into the surrounding dry ground. The small exposed faces drop toward the water. There is no separate outer wall lifting the whole rim above the path.

The wide view shows a gently flattened far bank, uneven side returns and a broad near arc. Individual stones interrupt the outline; their widths and shapes vary. The left and right sides are not perfect mirror images. The image can support a broadly rounded basin, but it does not establish a circle or a mathematically exact ellipse.

The basin extends across most of the frame and substantially into its depth. As rough **image proportions**, its visible outline spans about 0.85 of the frame width and 0.45 of the frame height. The far central inner stone edge lies near 0.36 of the image height, and the near central edge near 0.81, measured from the top. These are estimates of the drawn basin boundary, not a calibrated trace of the waterline beneath the stone faces. Perspective prevents converting these fractions directly into the pond's proportions on the ground.

Pebbles remain visible through much of the water, especially toward the foreground. The bed has local variation rather than reading as a featureless horizontal plate. Shallow marginal growth interrupts the waterline, while larger planting continues beyond the coping into the boundary beds. The plants and timber enclosure form a continuous garden edge rather than isolated objects around a freestanding pool.

## Relationships between the views

The garden-pond and waterwheel images retain the same broad shoreline, foreground stones, rear planting, timber boundary and camera composition. The wheel is added within the left side of that basin, with its supports in shallow water. Tools remain on the near dry stones.

The feed fitting changes with the project. In the empty pond view, its tip reaches farther right and lower in the image; in the wheel view, the outlet is raised and redirected over the wheel. That alteration can belong to installing the project. It does not require moving the pond or treating the outlet as an immovable feature of every scene.

The rescue looks obliquely along a bank, with dry ground at left and water opening across the right and foreground. Its near shoreline is therefore not expected to repeat the wide view's screen outline. The comfort view places the group farther onto dry ground, with the basin behind them. Those are compatible local relationships, but these illustrations do not supply measured camera positions or reveal every hidden stretch of shore.

## Scale supplied by the actions

In the rescue, the older children kneel beside one another on broad paving. Their knees, feet and weight remain on the bank while their arms reach Lyra. Barkley has standing room behind them, and Shadow occupies dry ground beside the edge. The scene requires considerably more supported space than a narrow decorative coping strip.

Lyra is low in the water with a bent leg visible. The waterline across her clothing does **not** establish waist-deep water for a standing child. Her posture, the reachable bank and the visibly shallow bottom must be considered together.

The recovery accommodates Cali holding Lyra, Kael beside them, Barkley and Shadow on one continuous area of dry paving. The children's bodies are forward of the pond, not balanced along its rim. This gives a useful relationship between occupied ground and the basin even though their exact distances remain unknown.

The wheel is a small project beside the planting and stones. Its size relative to the entire basin is visually constrained; neither the wheel nor the fence provides a reliable metre scale by itself. Exact pond dimensions, depth, bank width and camera heights remain choices to test against these scenes.
