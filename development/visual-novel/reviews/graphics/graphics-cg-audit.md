# Selected CG character and action audit

This is a fresh visual audit of **all 20 selected story/theme CGs**, not an adoption of the acceptance notes in `cg-character-review.json`. Recommendations below concern characters, familiar identity, physical interaction and depicted story action. Architecture, windows, lighting systems and background continuity have a separate owner. **Keep** means no independently justified character/action repair was found in this inspection; it is not a blanket production approval.

I inspected every complete composition, then native-pixel face crops across the set. I separately inspected the pond animals, Nibble, flute hands/instrument, rescue grasp, grief hand contact and embrace. Full-size rechecks covered the dome, arrival, seated treehouse trio and pond comfort. The contact-sheet face crops were not stretched or enlarged. Large close-up faces were assessed in their original composition as well: a close camera is not evidence that the child grew a larger head. File hashes below identify the actual inputs; later replacements need a new inspection.

## Priority findings

The strongest recurring defect is **facial construction/style drift**, rather than a new across-the-board body-scale error. The arrival and seated treehouse scenes give the girls conspicuously round, exposed eyes and very short nose/lower-face construction. The dome carries similarly glossy, very young-looking faces on its later-childhood bodies. These differ from the more grounded cheek, lid and mouth construction elsewhere. Repair the selected face regions against the agreed stage control, preserving the existing expressions and camera; changing iris hue alone cannot fix this.

The pond and grief paintings should retain their useful poses. In pond comfort, Kael is deeply crouched on his boots farther back while Cali kneels upright nearer the camera. His larger hands, forearms and torso still communicate the older sibling. The seated treehouse figures also have different hip/cushion positions and lean angles. Equalizing their crowns would introduce a new error.

Two concrete smaller checks remain: Shadow's visible ear silhouettes in the pond pair do not clearly reproduce her identifying notch; and the theme waterwheel is on dry planks under a spout rather than installed in the pond. The latter is compatible with a test phase, but not with the explicitly described completed installation.

## Cross-image follow-through

The final theme currently moves from later-childhood waterwheel/dome back to early-costume sketching/evening, then to later morning. This is a memory montage, so it does not contradict the source by itself. It does mean the theme cannot serve as a chronological age chart. Do not “correct” all close-up heads to a common pixel size or silently age every recalled moment to the outro stage.

For the recommended face work, hold eye aperture/lid construction, nose bridge/tip, cheek volume, jaw/chin and skin treatment together. Match the same **character and stage**, while allowing a smile, profile, lowered gaze and changing light. Compare every corrected face at full scene size with 03/13/16/19 and the final agreed production controls. A successful iris mask proves containment, not facial consistency.

The repair list intentionally preserves useful art and interaction. It does not call for rebuilding every scene, removing existing extras/familiars, replacing hand poses that already work, or inserting missing exposition to justify a picture. The currently selected images still need the separate environment audit and review after any replacements.
