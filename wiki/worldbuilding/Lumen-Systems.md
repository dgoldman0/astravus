# Lumen: Environment and Supporting Systems

Part of the [Lumen world specification](Lumen.md#world-specification). **Working canon, version 1 (September 2026).** These operating rules extend the existing setting. They define observable behavior and spatial needs while leaving some underlying technologies open. Numerical environmental targets are chosen design defaults, not measurements from the story.

## Gravity and orientation

Artificial gravity is an accepted fictional capability. Habitation normally feels Earthlike, with a **nominal 1 g** directed consistently downward through connected rooms, gardens, terraces and public routes. It acts through the occupied air volume as well as at the floor: rain falls, lanterns rise through buoyancy, and a climbing child remains subject to gravity above the paving.

Residents do not need a Core to experience gravity. Ordinary emotional changes do not disturb it. Lumen's structural and control tissues maintain the service, including during normal travel. Variable-gravity equipment may exist in enclosed specialist spaces, but everyday transit, the children's construction routes and Luxa are not automatically low-g environments. See [Artificial Gravity](Artificial-Gravity.md) for limits and the superseded VSB proposal.

All directly connected habitation shares a common down direction. Internal gravity does not imply a strong gravitational pull outside Lumen, permission to walk on the vacuum-facing hull, faster-than-light travel, or unlimited propulsion. The relation between the field and the drive remains a separate engineering question.

## Air, enclosure and sky

Habitation contains breathable, approximately Earthlike air. Use **about 100 kPa pressure, 21% oxygen, 20–25 °C and 40–65% relative humidity** as ordinary residential modelling defaults. Gardens may have locally different conditions. These are setting choices, not a medical assessment of engineered Astraviin biology.

The main city chamber and its adjoining occupied chambers are sealed within a deeper pressure-retaining body. Broad passages usually join them openly; closable boundaries allow a damaged section to be isolated. The city need not be one undivided pressure volume. Transparent-looking surfaces can be lighting membranes or internal glazing; they are not automatically the outer pressure skin.

**Lumen's sky** is the overhead environment experienced by residents: bright living membranes, optical layers, structural vaults, local skylights and intervening foliage. Some volumes show large-scale sky effects; intimate scenes mostly see dappled light and nearby ceilings. A crystal dome over garden planting can be an internal local enclosure. Rain can occur beneath that enclosure through its own environmental system.

The unfinished dome is inside breathable, gravity-served space. Its scaffolding is not an exposed exterior hull. Hull growth and unpressurized work occur beyond separate containment boundaries. The public construction edge can contain unfinished rooms and tools without forcing the existing children-and-familiars scenes into spacesuits.

The later observation deck has an actual protected optical view into space. Views through outer apertures can be shuttered when needed. A panorama used as visual reference does not establish that the whole body is transparent, or that every bedroom overlooks space.

## Day, light and weather

Use a **24-hour common civil day** for the reference model, with about 14 hours of daytime illumination including gradual dawn and dusk. The civil year is Earth-derived, approximately 365 days, and is not an orbital period. Local horticultural cycles can differ while public spaces retain a shared social clock. Seasonal celebrations do not require a planetary orbit or terrestrial winter weather.

Daylight comes from maintained illumination systems in the overhead structure and local apertures. Where useful, collected starlight can supplement them; inhabited daylight continues between stars. Light has direction and variation, producing shade beneath the oak and brighter planting areas nearby. This preserves sunflowers reaching for a skylight and the solar-powered model car: the model collects light, regardless of the primary source that ultimately powered the illumination.

Bioluminescent plants, local lamps, candles and lanterns provide the evening vocabulary. Small glowing plants are not tasked with supplying all crop energy or all daytime light. A resident may call the daylight “sunlight” without asserting that a nearby star directly illuminates every room.

Rain is real water, released by managed weather systems in garden and public landscape volumes. It wets exposed leaves, paths and the treehouse roof; it remains outside the sheltered upper room and its open window bays. Overhead structure can conceal fine distribution outlets and condensation collection. Natural-looking clouds are possible in larger chambers but are not necessary above each rainy scene.

Use gentle air circulation in everyday scenes. Airflow follows planted streets and returns through the structure; workshops have local extraction. Lumen can vary warmth, fragrance, light and humidity subtly while keeping climate suitable for residents and plants. Mood does not casually change air chemistry or turn a pleasant day into a dangerous environment.

## Water and landscape

Water circulates through reservoirs, supply channels, local features, drains, treatment systems and nutrient-recovery loops. Garden water and drinking water have distinct treatment paths. Decorative streams may reveal part of the circulation, but the entire city is not supplied by a single scenic pond.

The children's pond stays shallow with a low accessible edge and a broad dry bank. Other ponds remain available elsewhere in Maia's garden; using one basin for several VN shots is a production choice. A small recirculating inlet or bypass supplies the miniature waterwheel. The wheel demonstrates motion; it is not Lumen's power source.

Upper gardens require soil/root volume, irrigation and drainage. Water moving to lower terraces is collected rather than pouring through occupied rooms. Pumps return it to higher levels. Do not add a river, enormous lake or sea solely because one appears in an imaginary map, artwork or shared dream.

## Food, material and energy flows

| System | Spatial provision and operating rule | Still to quantify |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Food | Household gardens supplement dedicated cultivation chambers, fermentation and other biological food production. Routine work is largely automated; guilds direct cultivation and innovation. | Diet, crop mix, yield, lighting demand, reserves and familiar feed. |
| Nutrients and waste | Organics are recovered and processed; reusable materials return to workshops or growth systems. Medical and industrial waste have separate handling. | Recovery fractions, unavoidable losses and storage volume. |
| Water and air | Reservoirs, treatment tissue, atmospheric processing and circulation occupy lower/flanking chambers and local service routes. | Inventories, redundancy, treatment rates and crop/animal balance. |
| Primary energy | Stored fuel and conversion organs sustain Lumen independently of nearby sunlight. Stellar collection supplements supply when available. | Conversion mechanism, rated power, fuel chemistry and storage. |
| Heat | Heat moves through internal transport loops to external radiating surfaces; these extend outside the main-body dimensions. | Temperature, radiating area, deployment shape and operating limits. |
| Growth | New chambers consume material, energy and construction time. Parent Astravii and later resource expeditions supply external inputs alongside recycling. | Mass, growth rate, structural loads and material sources. |
| Movement | Drive structures occupy an aft region separated from habitation and docking approaches. Internal comfort is maintained during normal manoeuvres. | Thrust, acceleration, propellant, travel speeds and flight profiles. |

The choice of stored-fuel operation and external heat rejection establishes physical places and finite resource flows, not a validated power plant. The atlas's cultivation area is a capacity allowance to be tested, not proof that the entire food and energy budget closes. Artificial gravity is the explicit physical leeway; it does not make the remaining accounting disappear.

## Grown structure and made objects

Lumen grows its load-bearing and responsive tissues with guidance from inhabitants and automated systems. People also cut timber, tighten bolts, weld, assemble devices, patch roofs and install tools. A scaffold can guide growth, support a conventional assembly, or provide access during maintenance. Manufactured and grown components coexist.

Bulk architectural changes happen over construction timescales, with routes and services maintained around occupied areas. A new bloom or changing mural can express Lumen's attention without implying that it can grow a whole furnished home instantly. Old rooms and landmarks can be maintained for centuries while the surrounding body expands.

The Core network is distributed through living structure. Do not add a single exposed glowing “brain chamber” merely because inhabitants have personal Cores. Details of computation, nanomachines and the biological endpoint of transcendence remain open; furnishings should not imply an unestablished mechanism.

## Access, care and the limits of protection

Homes, streets and services work for children, visitors and residents without adult Core interfaces. Touch controls, physical tools and spoken requests coexist with thought-based interfaces. Environmental requests are local and mediated: one person's preferred temperature does not automatically alter the whole city or someone else's home.

The Sanctuary includes preparation, private receiving/ceremony rooms, counseling and staff areas. Clinical care and Core Integration have suitable spaces nearby but do not share an invented birth apparatus. During transcendence, people can continue receiving care at home or in appropriate care rooms; sleep lasting days or months has practical support.

Lumen is attentive but does not remove every opportunity for a scraped knee, a shallow-water fright or a risky climb. Protective systems are not omnipotent. Joren's fatal expedition remains a rare malfunction during a nearby-moon research trip; this specification assigns no new cause, extra casualty or gravity failure to it.

## Travel and communication

Local craft connect Lumen to moons, resources and visiting vessels. Inter-Astravii journeys can be much longer. Place the arrival concourse and side-facing docking chambers so passengers and freight can reach the city without crossing propulsion work areas.

The nearby-moon expedition establishes that Lumen can be in a planetary system during Book I. It does not fix that planet's name, orbit, gravity or resource composition. Family visits to Aurora and Nyx do not establish a universal travel duration; Lyra explicitly describes some journeys taking years.

Messages can be sent and received across distance, but neither instantaneous interstellar communication nor faster-than-light travel is established here. Calista's request for regular contact and the received return message need no added technical explanation. The range and transport of shared consciousness between Astravii remain open; dream contact should not silently become a physical doorway.
