# Lumen: Population and Growth

Part of the [Lumen world specification](Lumen.md#world-specification). **Working canon, version 1 (September 2026).** Numerical values below are new worldbuilding decisions, rounded to support consistent writing and modelling; they are not census figures extracted from the manuscript. [Continuity and decision records](Lumen-Continuity.md) distinguish these additions from inherited facts.

## Reference dates and population

Use **C+0** for Calista's First Breath. Negative dates precede it. **C+10** is the representative Book I layout, not a claim that every childhood scene happens at ten. Keep early and later childhood appearances and events distinct.

| Reference | Embodied residents, approximately | World state |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Founding, around C−100 | 3,000 | A settlement supplied and seeded by Aurora and Nyx; the Tree of Echoes arrives already ancient. |
| C+10 | 10,000 | Book I reference city; the first locally born generation is the majority. |
| C+50 | 14,000 | Calista and Aris welcome Elara; more homes and workshops occupy adjoining terraces. |
| C+125 | 22,000 | Mature local culture and further expansion; Elara is 75. |
| C+150, illustrative later snapshot | 26,000 | Radiant Fields in use during Calista's extended transition years. |

The founding date is an approximate new baseline. Lumen is about 110 years old at the Book I reference, still young beside its approximately thousand-year-old parents. “In its infancy” describes an Astravus's development, not an infant human's timescale. The final date of Calista's transcendence remains unspecified. C+150 is a modelling snapshot within that open period, not a newly dated scene or death.

Use roughly ±20% around the resident estimates for planning variants. Use the central estimates in the shared model; do not quietly combine a high-population variant with the smallest housing and gathering allowances.

## What a resident count includes

- Count each embodied person once, including children, adults before and after Core Integration, and people spending extended periods asleep during transcendence.
- Count a resident temporarily away on an expedition at their usual home, not again as a permanent resident of the destination. Maintain a separate present-onboard count for immediate provisioning and visitors.
- Keep fully transcended minds in a separate collective-memory category. Core Integration at 35 does not remove someone from the resident count.
- Keep familiars in a separate animal population and provisioning account. They require habitat and care even though they are not included in the human-sized dwelling estimate.
- A transition does not instantly free a bedroom. Continue reserving bodily care space for as long as it is needed; the final biological fate of the body is not specified here.

The size of Lumen's founding consciousness, the number of fully transcended minds, and the familiar census remain open. Do not derive them from the resident count.

## A young community with older people

For C+10, use this illustrative allocation when populating streets, homes, classes and guilds:

| Age | Residents | Share |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| 0–14 | 1,400 | 14% |
| 15–34 | 1,800 | 18% |
| 35–124 | 5,200 | 52% |
| 125 and older | 1,600 | 16% |
| **Total** | **10,000** | **100%** |

This is a layout allowance, not a measured distribution or a prescribed age of transcendence. It leaves approximately a third of residents without adult Core interfaces. Physical controls, accessible paths and ordinary communication therefore remain essential.

A compatible origin allocation is approximately 6,500 people from the first locally born generation, 1,500 from subsequent locally born generations, and 2,000 residents born elsewhere. The 125+ residents necessarily arrived from elsewhere under this founding baseline. These origin and age tables describe the same population, not additive populations.

“First locally born generation” distinguishes the children of the settling population from their later descendants. Its births span decades; it is not a single school-year cohort. Different parental ages and overlapping generations make the boundaries approximate. The phrase describes early Lumen and must not remain a permanent demographic condition as Elara's generation grows up. This does not assign any named parent's birthplace, age at migration, or genetic relationship.

## Households and housing

Book I needs about **3,000 occupied residential groupings**, averaging roughly 3.3 residents. These include people living alone, partners without children, co-resident friends and families of different sizes. An economic household may connect several residences; these are not 3,000 necessarily independent enterprises.

Count adult partnerships separately from families and residential groupings. A **constellation is an adult romantic/peer relationship**, not a family or household. The manuscript describes two or three adult partners as common. Calista's parents form a five-adult constellation; separately, their family includes the three children and familiars. Only the five adult partners are members of that constellation. Their large family home is not the universal floor-plan template, and the residential count does not establish a constellation count.

Reserve approximately **0.95 km² of residential interior floor area** at C+10, or 95 m² per resident including shared domestic rooms and residential circulation. Gardens, substantial guild workshops and public paths have separate allocations in the [atlas](Lumen-Atlas.md#space-budget). This gives an average residential grouping about 317 m², distributed unevenly. Calista's childhood household can initially be tested at 450–650 m² including its music, library and small workshop spaces. These local dimensions are modelling targets, subordinate to fitting the existing rooms convincingly.

Additional family homes at C+35–50 are added in the same broader neighborhood. Calista's new household, Kael's household and the original family home remain distinct places. Regular visits do not require their residents to share a single building forever.

## Growth arithmetic

Use:

`later residents = earlier residents + First Breaths + arrivals − departures − completed transitions out of embodied residence − deaths`

Growth is slow by human settlement standards but persists for many decades. Few deaths do not imply unlimited population growth: births, migration and transcendence all matter. Family examples are not a fertility survey.

The following **illustrative accounting**, in thousands of people, shows one way to reach the rounded baselines. It does not establish exact birth quotas, migration events or transition dates.

| Interval | Start | First Breaths | Arrivals | Departures | Completed transitions and rare deaths combined | End |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Founding → C+10 | 3.0 | 9.0 | 1.0 | 0.6 | 2.4 | 10.0 |
| C+10 → C+50 | 10.0 | 5.2 | 1.2 | 0.4 | 2.0 | 14.0 |
| C+50 → C+125 | 14.0 | 11.5 | 2.0 | 0.5 | 5.0 | 22.0 |
| C+125 → illustrative C+150 | 22.0 | 6.5 | 0.5 | 0.2 | 2.8 | 26.0 |

The combined exit column is overwhelmingly transcendence; it does not introduce additional named casualties or make death commonplace. The origin allocation above also requires most early exits to be among settlers and older arrivals, which is compatible with their greater ages. Exact genealogies remain open.

At C+10–50 the example averages 130 First Breaths annually, around two or three a week across the Sanctuary. That is enough for staffed rooms and several families preparing concurrently, without turning each arrival into an event physically attended by everyone aboard. First Breath and the annual community festival have different gathering scales.
